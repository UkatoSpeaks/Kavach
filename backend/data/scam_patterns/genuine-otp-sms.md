## How it works

When you log in, make a payment or change settings, the bank, card issuer or app sends a
one-time password (OTP) by SMS. The message gives the OTP, what it is for (merchant, amount
or action), how long it is valid, and a warning not to share it with anyone, including bank
staff.

## Signs it is genuine

- You just started the login, payment or change yourself.
- The merchant and amount match what you are doing.
- It clearly says not to share the OTP, and that the bank never asks for it.
- Sent from the organisation's registered sender ID.

## Example messages

- English: "845120 is your OTP for a transaction of INR 2,399.00 at AMAZON on your card
  ending 5521. Valid for 5 minutes. Do not share this OTP with anyone."
- Hinglish style: "Your login OTP for net banking is 339274. Bank kabhi OTP nahi maangta, kisi
  ke saath share na karein."
- Hindi: "आपका ओटीपी 562318 है, यह 10 मिनट तक मान्य है। इसे किसी के साथ साझा न करें, बैंक
  कभी भी ओटीपी नहीं मांगता।"

## What to do

- Enter the OTP only on the screen where you started the action.
- Never tell the OTP to anyone who calls or messages you.

## When it could be a scam

- If you receive an OTP you did not ask for and someone then calls asking for it, it is a
  fraud attempt. Do not share it.
