## How it works

You receive a message saying a traffic e-challan (fine) is pending against your vehicle and
must be paid within hours to avoid court action, licence suspension or extra penalty. The
link leads to a look-alike payment page that steals card or UPI details, or downloads an app
that reads your messages.

## Red flags

- A link to pay the challan that is not the official government traffic portal.
- Domain names that mix words like "echallan", "parivahan" or "traffic" with odd endings
  such as .top or .xyz, or short links.
- A link that downloads an .apk file.
- Threats of court action within 24 hours.

## Example messages

- English: "Traffic violation notice: e-challan of Rs 1,000 is due on your vehicle. Pay
  today to avoid court proceedings. Click the link to pay."
- Hinglish: "Aapki gaadi ka e-challan ₹500 pending hai, 24 ghante me bharo warna court case
  hoga. Link pe click karke payment karein."
- Hindi: "आपके वाहन पर ₹2,000 का ई-चालान बकाया है। कोर्ट की कार्रवाई से बचने के लिए आज ही दिए
  गए लिंक से भुगतान करें।"

## What to do

- Do not pay through a link in a message.
- Check pending challans yourself on the official government portal by typing the address or
  using the official app.
- Never install an .apk file sent in a message.

## Where to report

- If money was taken, call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 immediately and file a
  complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Also inform your bank.
- Report the fraud SMS or sender on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi).
