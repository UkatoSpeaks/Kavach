## How it works

Fraudsters paste their own QR sticker over a shop's, parking lot's or fuel station's QR code,
or send a message claiming a society, school, landlord or business has a "new" QR for
payments. People scan it thinking they are paying the right party, and the money goes to the
fraudster. The victim often only finds out when the real payee says they were never paid.

## Red flags

- A QR sticker that looks pasted over another one, or is loose or damaged.
- The name shown after scanning is a person or an unrelated name, not the shop or organisation.
- A forwarded message saying "our bank account has changed, pay only to this new QR".
- Pressure to pay a fee or fine quickly by scanning.

## Example messages

- English: "Dear residents, our society maintenance account has changed. Please pay this
  month's maintenance only by scanning the new QR code attached."
- Hinglish: "Parking ka payment ab naye QR se hoga, purana band ho gaya hai. Yeh QR scan karke
  ₹100 bhej do."
- Hindi: "स्कूल की फीस अब इस नए QR कोड से ही जमा करें, पुराना खाता बंद कर दिया गया है।"

## What to do

- After scanning, check that the payee name matches the shop or organisation before entering
  your PIN.
- Confirm any "account changed" message by calling the organisation on a number you already
  know.
- Tell the shop owner if their QR looks tampered with.

## Where to report

- If money was taken, call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 immediately and file a
  complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Also inform your bank.
- Report the fraud SMS or WhatsApp message on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi).
