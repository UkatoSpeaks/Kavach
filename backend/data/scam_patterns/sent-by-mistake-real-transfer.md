## How it works

Sometimes money really does arrive in your account from an unknown sender, followed by a
request to return it to a *different* UPI ID, number or account. The money may have been
stolen from another victim. Sending it onward can make your account part of the fraud trail,
and your account may be frozen during an investigation. The original victim's complaint can
also lead to the amount being recovered from you.

## Red flags

- The person asks you to return the money to a different account or UPI ID than the one it
  came from.
- They refuse to go through their bank and insist you send it yourself, quickly.
- The sender's name on the credit does not match the person contacting you.
- Several small "mistaken" credits from different people.

## Example messages

- English: "I sent ₹7,000 to you by mistake. Please return it to my other UPI ID
  because my first account is blocked."
- Hinglish: "Mere dost ke account se aapko galti se paise aa gaye, woh wapas mere Paytm pe
  bhej do, uska account band hai."
- Hindi: "गलती से आपके खाते में पैसे आ गए हैं, कृपया उन्हें इस दूसरी UPI आईडी पर लौटा दें।"

## What to do

- Do not forward the money to any other account.
- Contact your bank, report the unknown credit, and let the bank handle any reversal.
- Keep screenshots of the messages.

## Where to report

- Call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 or file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in if you
  think the money came from a fraud. Inform your bank.
- Report the calling number or WhatsApp message on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi).
