## How it works

You get an SMS that looks like a bank credit alert saying money was deposited into your
account. Minutes later a stranger calls or messages, saying they sent it to you by mistake
and begging you to return it, often with an emotional story like a hospital bill or school
fees. The SMS was fake, sent from an ordinary mobile number, and no money ever reached you.
If you "return" it, you lose your own money.

## Red flags

- A credit alert from a 10-digit mobile number instead of your bank's sender ID.
- A call or message about the "mistake" arrives right after the SMS.
- Emotional pressure and urgency: "please return fast, it is for my mother's operation".
- Your bank app or balance does not show the credit.

## Example messages

- English: "Sorry, I transferred ₹4,500 to your account by mistake while paying my brother.
  Please send it back to this number on GPay, it is urgent."
- Hinglish: "Bhaiya galti se aapke number pe ₹2,000 chale gaye, please wapas bhej do, bahut
  zaroori hai."
- Hindi: "माफ़ कीजिए, गलती से आपके खाते में ₹3,000 भेज दिए। कृपया उसी नंबर पर वापस कर दीजिए।"

## What to do

- Check your balance in your bank app. Do not trust an SMS or screenshot.
- Do not send money back yourself. If a real wrong credit happened, the sender should ask
  their own bank to raise a reversal.
- Block the number.

## Where to report

- If money was taken, call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 immediately and file a
  complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Also inform your bank.
- Report the fake SMS and the calling number on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi).
