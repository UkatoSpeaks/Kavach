## How it works

A "buyer" contacts you about something you are selling online, such as furniture, a bike or
a phone. They agree to the price quickly and say they will pay an advance. Instead of paying,
they send a QR code on WhatsApp and ask you to scan it to receive the money. Scanning the QR
opens a payment screen; entering your UPI PIN pays them. They may claim it failed and send
another QR for a larger amount.

## Red flags

- Being asked to scan a QR code to receive money. Scanning a QR is always for paying.
- The buyer claims to be in the army, a government officer, or out of town.
- A QR code with an amount already filled in.
- Repeated "try again" requests after the first payment.

## Example messages

- English: "I want to buy your sofa. I am sending you a QR code for ₹6,000 advance, scan it
  with any UPI app and the money will be credited to you."
- Hinglish: "Aapka scooty ka ad dekha, main le lunga. Advance ke liye QR bhej raha hoon,
  scan karke PIN daalo paise aa jayenge."
- Hindi: "मैं आपका फ़ोन खरीदूँगा। एडवांस के लिए QR कोड भेजा है, उसे स्कैन करें तो पैसे आपके खाते
  में आ जाएँगे।"

## What to do

- Never scan a QR code to receive money. Give the buyer your UPI ID instead.
- Confirm any credit in your bank app, not from a screenshot the buyer sends.
- Stop the conversation and block the number.

## Where to report

- If money was taken, call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 immediately and file a
  complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Also inform your bank.
- Report the fraud call or WhatsApp number on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi).
